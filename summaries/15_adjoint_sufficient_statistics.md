# Chapter 15. Adjoint Sufficient Statistics

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *On Hilbert's 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 15. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *On Hilbert's 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation*, edition 1, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23327515. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/hilbert6/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/hilbert6.
<!-- cite:end -->

Which statistic of the data should generative or approximate Bayesian computation (GBC, ABC) condition on when the unknown is the initial condition of a dissipative partial differential equation observed with noise? For linear equations such as heat and Stokes, the adjoint statistic $\Phi^\top Y$, the time zero value of the backward adjoint equation driven by the data, is exactly sufficient together with the design. For nonlinear forward maps (reaction diffusion, two dimensional Navier Stokes) the chapter defines a cross fitted one step efficient score summary, proves it, under rate conditions on its preliminary estimates, root N equivalent to the efficient centering of Nickl's Bernstein von Mises theorem, and bounds its error in Sobolev norms. It gives Lindley information gain identities, a Kullback Leibler form of the Bernstein von Mises theorem and an entropic van Trees bound, identifies the adjoint statistic with partial least squares (PLS) in the linear Gaussian model, and shows that the posterior mean summary is asymptotically sufficient under a uniform Bernstein von Mises hypothesis, which the book leaves open. Rao Blackwell and transport entropy bounds bracket the sufficiency gap of a linear summary in the linear Gaussian model, dissipation lowers the effective dimension of the heat and Stokes posteriors, and a generator trained on the raw data, accurate uniformly over a Hölder class, needs a number of simulations growing faster than any exponential in $N$.

## From the literature

Bernstein von Mises theorems for time evolution equations (Nickl 2024) and for the two dimensional Navier Stokes equations (Konen and Nickl 2026); the expected information gain (Lindley 1956) and information as expected utility for diffusions (Polson and Roberts 1993); the posterior mean as a summary (Fearnhead and Prangle 2012; Jiang, Wu, Zheng and Wong 2017); agreement of ABC selection criteria (Hoffmann and Onnela 2026); the single index theorem (Brillinger 1983; Li and Duan 1989); deep partial least squares (Polson, Sokolov and Xu 2021); the transport entropy inequality (Talagrand 1996); likelihood informed subspaces (Cui et al. 2014) and optimal low rank posterior approximations (Spantini et al. 2015); the 4DVar adjoint (Le Dimet and Talagrand 1986); cross fitting (Chernozhukov et al. 2018).

## New results

The catalogued results are the authors' own; some first appeared in their unpublished working paper on the solution manifold.

- [Adjoint representation of the sufficient statistic](../RESULTS.md#adjoint_representation_of_the_sufficient_statistic) (Lemma 15.1, page 206; proved): For a linear evolution equation that is diagonal in a fixed basis, the data statistic that is sufficient for the initial state is the coefficient vector of the time zero value of the solution of the backward adjoint equation driven by the data as point sources. New in this book.
- [Exact sufficiency, linear case](../RESULTS.md#exact_sufficiency_linear_case) (Proposition 15.2, page 207; proved): In the linear model with a Gaussian prior the adjoint statistic is sufficient, the posterior given it is the full data posterior, and the conditional quantile map is exactly affine with slope equal to the posterior covariance. New in this book.
- [Equivalence of the one step efficient score summary with the efficient centering](../RESULTS.md#equivalence_with_the_efficient_centering) (Proposition 15.5, page 210; proved): Under explicit regularity, rate and Lipschitz conditions, the cross fitted one step efficient score summary agrees with the efficient centering of Nickl's Bernstein von Mises theorem to order smaller than the root N scale in a negative Sobolev norm, so it is a computable center of the Gaussian limit of the posterior. New in this book.
- [The one step summary in Sobolev norms](../RESULTS.md#the_summary_in_sobolev_norms) (Proposition 15.6, page 213; proved): Under bounds on the linearizations and a rate for the preliminary estimates in a negative Sobolev norm, the error of the one step summary is bounded at an explicit polynomial rate in every Sobolev norm from index minus one to gamma, a rate that tends to zero for indices below gamma minus one, which gives the L2 and high Sobolev bounds that the generator theorem assumes of the summary. New in this book.
- [Preliminary estimates with the rate in $H^{-1}$](../RESULTS.md#preliminary_estimates_rate_in_h_minus_one) (Lemma 15.7, page 215; proved): A preliminary estimate bounded in a high Sobolev norm whose forward image converges at the rate delta N in L2 also converges at that rate in H minus one, interpolation then gives the whole family of Sobolev rates the summary needs, and the posterior mean computed on half the sample satisfies them. New in this book.
- [Sobolev rates of the one step summary under the efficient centering hypotheses](../RESULTS.md#summary_sobolev_rate_under_centering_hypotheses) (Corollary 15.9, page 217; proved): Under the hypotheses of the equivalence of the one step summary with the efficient centering, the summary has the same Sobolev rates as the earlier proposition, plus a term of smaller order than the root N scale multiplied by a power of N that grows with s and k, so that this term need not be small relative to the root N scale. New in this book.
- [The information operator of the reaction diffusion class](../RESULTS.md#the_information_operator_of_the_reaction_diffusion_class) (Lemma 15.11, page 219; proved): For the reaction diffusion class, the information operator differs from the heat equation one by a correction that gains regularity, the linearization has a quadratic remainder, and the summary weights depend on the linearization point in a Lipschitz way, all uniformly on Sobolev balls. New in this book.
- [The one step summary in Sobolev norms: reaction diffusion](../RESULTS.md#the_summary_in_sobolev_norms_reaction_diffusion) (Proposition 15.12, page 219; proved): For the reaction diffusion class the one step summary has the L2 and high Sobolev norm bounds that the generator theorem assumes, for every admissible choice of the preliminary estimates. New in this book.
- [The regularized summary on $J_N$ modes: heat and Stokes](../RESULTS.md#the_regularized_summary_on_modes_heat_and_stokes) (Proposition 15.13, page 220; proved): For the heat and Stokes equations the regularized summary on J N modes, which is the truncated posterior mean, is root N equivalent to Nickl's efficient centering in a negative Sobolev norm whatever the pilot estimate, and its posterior has the Gaussian Bernstein von Mises limit. New in this book.
- [The regularized summary under a fixed prior, and the untruncated posteriors](../RESULTS.md#regularized_summary_fixed_prior_untruncated_posteriors) (Proposition 15.14, page 225; proved): For the heat and Stokes equations the root N equivalence of the regularized summary with the efficient centering also holds under a fixed Gaussian prior and, under the rescaled prior, for the untruncated posterior, and under the fixed prior for its untruncated posterior when the prior exponent exceeds the dimension; when the true initial condition meets the hypotheses for both priors, the regularized summaries under the two priors are root N equivalent, and so are the untruncated posterior means when that exponent exceeds the dimension. New in this book.
- [The regularized summary on $J_N$ modes: reaction diffusion](../RESULTS.md#the_regularized_summary_on_modes_reaction_diffusion) (Proposition 15.16, page 228; proved): For the reaction diffusion class the cross fitted regularized summary on J N modes is root N equivalent in a negative Sobolev norm to the efficient centering, to the posterior mean and, under the hypotheses of the equivalence result, to the one step summary. New in this book.
- [Expected utility form of the Bernstein von Mises limit](../RESULTS.md#expected_utility_form_of_the_bernstein_von_mises_limit) (Proposition 15.18, page 231; proved): For the heat and Stokes equations the information gain from the data about the first J modes is given exactly by explicit formulas in the per mode ratios of data information to prior precision, its prior predictive expectation is Lindley's expected information gain, and under the rescaled prior only a vanishing fraction of the retained modes is strongly informative. New in this book.
- [Exact identities for the information gain under any forward map](../RESULTS.md#exact_identities_for_any_forward_map) (Lemma 15.21, page 233; proved): For any measurable forward map and a Gaussian product prior, the realized information gain is the Kullback Leibler divergence of the Gaussian approximation of the posterior from the prior plus a nonnegative non Gaussianity term, and, when the forward map has a finite prior second moment in supremum norm, the expected gain is the mutual information, which equals half the expected log determinant of the prior covariance times the posterior precision plus the expected non Gaussianity term. New in this book.
- [Kullback Leibler form of the Bernstein von Mises theorem](../RESULTS.md#kullback_leibler_form_of_the_bernstein_von_mises_theorem) (Theorem 15.22, page 234; proved): Under the conditions of Nickl's Bernstein von Mises theorem, the posterior is close in Kullback Leibler divergence, in probability, to the Gaussian posterior of the model linearized at the truth. New in this book.
- [Realized information gain of the posterior with the efficient information at the truth](../RESULTS.md#realized_information_gain_with_efficient_information) (Corollary 15.23, page 236; proved): Under the hypotheses of the Kullback Leibler form of the Bernstein von Mises theorem, the non Gaussianity term of the information gain tends to zero uniformly in the number of modes, and for a fixed number of modes the realized gain is given up to o(1) in probability by the Gaussian formula with the efficient information at the truth. New in this book.
- [Kullback Leibler form of the Bernstein von Mises theorem for the Navier Stokes equations](../RESULTS.md#kullback_leibler_bernstein_von_mises_navier_stokes) (Proposition 15.24, page 236; proved): The Kullback Leibler form of the Bernstein von Mises theorem and its corollary on the realized information gain also hold for the two dimensional Navier Stokes equations with the prior of Konen and Nickl and smoothness exponent above twelve. New in this book.
- [Entropic van Trees bound](../RESULTS.md#entropic_van_trees_bound) (Proposition 15.25, page 238; proved): For a forward map that is continuously differentiable in a fixed number of modes, the expected information gain is bounded above by a log determinant of the prior averaged Fisher information, and, under the rescaled prior with exponent above the dimension and continuity, polynomial growth and invertibility conditions on the Fisher information at the center, by the same expression with the Fisher information at the center of the prior, up to o(1). New in this book.
- [Reaction diffusion: the expected gain is set at the center of the prior](../RESULTS.md#reaction_diffusion_expected_gain_at_prior_center) (Proposition 15.26, page 239; proved): For reaction diffusion equations with a fixed number of modes the expected information gain is bounded, up to o(1), by the gain computed with the information at the center of the prior, with equality when the reaction term vanishes near zero, and not by the information at the true initial condition, and for some such equations the information at the truth overstates the gain by an amount that does not vanish. New in this book.
- [Quadratic variation and the sufficient statistic](../RESULTS.md#quadratic_variation_and_the_sufficient_statistic) (Proposition 15.28, page 243; proved): With unknown noise variance the adjoint statistic together with the squared norm of the data is sufficient, and for an observed diffusion path the diffusion coefficient is identified by quadratic variation while the adjoint evolution driven by the data remains the sufficient statistic for the initial state. New in this book.
- [The one step summary under four ABC criteria: Bayes risk optimal up to o(1), all four attained in the J mode Gaussian projection](../RESULTS.md#one_step_summary_under_four_abc_criteria) (Proposition 15.29, page 244; conditional on the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption (Assumption U) and the uniform moment bound (MB)): Under the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption and a moment bound (which the book proves only for the linear Gaussian model), the full data bound four approximate Bayesian computation criteria (expected posterior entropy, Fisher information determinant, Bayes risk, information gain) over J dimensional statistics, the one step summary attains the Bayes risk optimum up to o(1), and it attains all four exactly in the J mode Gaussian projection. The agreement of these criteria in the large sample limit, for independent identically distributed data and a finite dimensional parameter, is due to Hoffmann and Onnela (2026). New in this book.
- [The adjoint statistic is the PLS projection](../RESULTS.md#the_adjoint_statistic_is_the_pls_projection) (Proposition 15.32, page 252; proved): In the linear model with a Gaussian prior the partial least squares scores computed from the raw data are exactly the coordinates of the sufficient adjoint statistic, so one PLS component per parameter coordinate suffices. New in this book.
- [Deep PLS structure of the GBC summary](../RESULTS.md#deep_pls_structure_of_the_gbc_summary) (Proposition 15.34, page 253; conditional on the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption (Assumption U), for the posterior given the scores alone): For a nonlinear forward map the first stage of the one step summary is a PLS step with directions given by the linearized forward map, and under the stated hypotheses, including the open uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption for the posterior given the scores, the map from the scores to the posterior is asymptotically the identity plus a scale. New in this book.
- [Jiang Wong under a uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption: the posterior mean summary is asymptotically sufficient](../RESULTS.md#jiang_wong_under_bernstein_von_mises) (Theorem 15.38, page 256; conditional on the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption (Assumption U)): If a Bernstein von Mises theorem holds uniformly over prior typical truths, then conditioning on the posterior mean alone, or on any summary root N equivalent to it, loses nothing at the root N scale: the recentered posterior given the summary and the recentered posterior given all of the data have the same Gaussian limit in Wasserstein distance. New in this book.
- [GBC with the posterior mean summary](../RESULTS.md#gbc_with_the_posterior_mean_summary) (Corollary 15.40, page 258; conditional on the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption (Assumption U), for the root N statement only): The target of generative Bayesian computation that conditions only on the posterior mean has, at every sample size, the exact posterior mean and the averaged posterior covariance (by the tower property and the law of total variance), and under a uniform Bernstein von Mises theorem it is within $o(N^{-1/2})$ of the posterior in Wasserstein distance. New in this book.
- [Sufficiency gap in the linear Gaussian model: Rao Blackwell lower bound and transport entropy upper bound](../RESULTS.md#sufficiency_gap) (Theorem 15.41, page 258; proved): For any linear summary in the linear Gaussian model, the Wasserstein distance between the posterior given the summary and the posterior given all of the data is bracketed, on average over the data, by two quantities computable before any simulation, one from the difference of the posterior covariances and one from the mutual information deficit, and both vanish exactly when the summary is Bayes sufficient, as the adjoint statistic is. New in this book.
- [Sufficiency gap beyond the linear model](../RESULTS.md#sufficiency_gap_beyond_the_linear_model) (Proposition 15.42, page 260; proved): If the posteriors given all of the data and given a summary are each within $o(N^{-1/2})$, in Wasserstein distance and on average under a coupling, of the corresponding posteriors of one linear Gaussian model, then the root N sufficiency gap of the summary is bracketed by the Rao Blackwell and transport entropy bounds of that Gaussian model; for the heat and Stokes equations and a truncated adjoint statistic the bounds are explicit. New in this book.
- [The Gaussian comparison models admitted by the first hypothesis of the nonlinear sufficiency gap bounds](../RESULTS.md#sufficiency_gap_admissible_gaussian_models) (Proposition 15.43, page 261; conditional on clauses (U1) and (U2) of the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption, and (U3) for part (ii)): Under a uniform Bernstein von Mises theorem, a linear Gaussian model can match the full data posterior at the root N scale only if its posterior covariance matches the Bernstein von Mises covariance on average over the prior; for a nonlinear forward map this forces a constant limit covariance under a fixed prior, and the inverse information at the prior mean rather than at the truth under the rescaled prior. New in this book.
- [A sufficient condition for the nonlinear sufficiency gap hypotheses in terms of the centering](../RESULTS.md#sufficiency_gap_hypotheses_from_centering_clt) (Lemma 15.44, page 263; conditional on clauses (U1) to (U3) of the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption): Under a uniform Bernstein von Mises theorem and a prior that contracts to zero, both hypotheses of the nonlinear sufficiency gap bounds follow, for a Gaussian model whose posterior covariance matches the limit at the prior mean, from a central limit statement for the conditional law of the centering given the summary. New in this book.
- [A two coordinate model in which the sufficiency gap hypotheses depend on the summary](../RESULTS.md#two_coordinate_model_sufficiency_gap_depends_on_summary) (Proposition 15.45, page 264; counterexample, proved): In a model with two parameters, a quadratic forward map and a prior contracting to zero, the Gaussian model at the prior mean matches the full data posterior at the root N scale while the Gaussian model at the truth need not, when the prior scale $s_N$ satisfies $\sqrt Ns_N^2\to\infty$, the posterior given the first coordinate of the adjoint statistic at the prior mean stays at a root N distance tending to infinity from every Gaussian posterior given a linear statistic, and the first coordinate of the maximum likelihood estimator satisfies both hypotheses of the nonlinear sufficiency gap bounds. New in this book.
- [Effective dimension under dissipation](../RESULTS.md#effective_dimension_under_dissipation) (Theorem 15.46, page 267; conditional on Assumption U and other hypotheses of the quantile generator theorem, in part (ii) for nonlinear maps): For the heat and Stokes equations on the torus with a power law prior, the number of modes in which the data at least halve the prior variance grows like a power of the sample size divided by viscosity times observation time, so dissipation lowers the effective dimension; for a nonlinear forward map, under a uniform Bernstein von Mises hypothesis and assumptions on the training residuals and the summary, a generator need only learn the posterior scale on those modes (in the linear model it needs no training), and the cost of using the prior spread beyond them is bounded explicitly. New in this book.
- [Sufficiency versus the raw data: simulation cost of a generator trained without a summary](../RESULTS.md#sufficiency_versus_the_raw_data) (Theorem 15.47, page 269; conditional on Assumption U and other hypotheses of the quantile generator theorem, for the second claim only): A generator trained on the raw observations and required to be accurate uniformly over a Hölder class of conditional quantile maps needs a number of simulations that grows faster than any exponential in the sample size, while the hybrid generator trained on the sufficient adjoint statistic needs only a number of simulations that grows faster than the effective dimension, conditionally on a uniform Bernstein von Mises hypothesis and assumptions on the training residuals and the summary. New in this book.
