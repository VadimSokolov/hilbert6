# Chapter 16. Transfer, Generators and Localized Training

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *On Hilbert's 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 16. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *On Hilbert's 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation*, edition 1, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23327515. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/hilbert6/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/hilbert6.
<!-- cite:end -->

How accurate must a generative Bayesian computation (GBC) generator be for its output to inherit the Bernstein von Mises (BvM) theorem of the exact posterior in a nonlinear PDE inverse problem, and how should its training pairs be drawn? In Nickl's framework for reaction diffusion equations and the Konen Nickl theory for two dimensional Navier Stokes, the transfer theorem shows that Wasserstein accuracy $o(N^{-1/2})$ and concentration on the localization set pass the Gaussian limit, exact credible band coverage and, for Navier Stokes, local asymptotic minimax efficiency for bounded losses to the GBC posterior. An identity skip quantile generator with a closed form Gaussian head is that accurate once $J/M\to0$ (summary dimension $J$, simulations $M$), conditionally on a uniform BvM assumption, a whitened residual assumption and a summary error bound. A minimax lower bound over monotone Hölder classes of conditional quantile maps shows that uniform accuracy needs at least of order $N^{1+J/(2\beta)}$ simulations, and local quantile estimators attain the exponent $\beta/(2\beta+J)$ (for $\beta>2$ with bounded densities). Under Nickl's conditions on $L^\infty$ balls, unverified for any forward map, a sparse deep ReLU network prior gives contraction in $H^{-1}$. For localized training it derives the exact residual law in the linear Gaussian model, a center and rescale rule, and proves the whitened residual assumption under a localized proposal.

## From the literature

- BvM theorems for time evolution equations (Nickl 2024) and for two dimensional Navier Stokes, with coverage and a local asymptotic minimax bound (Konen and Nickl 2026; Konen 2026); contraction for nonlinear inverse problems (Nickl 2023).
- Convolution and minimax theorems (van der Vaart and Wellner 2023), Le Cam's lemmas (van der Vaart 1998), Gaussian coverage (Giné and Nickl 2016).
- Covariance estimation (Vershynin 2018), empirical Wasserstein bounds (Bobkov and Ledoux 2019), Assouad's lemma (Tsybakov 2009), VC major empirical processes (Baraud 2016), Glaeser's inequality (Glaeser 1963), ReLU quantile regression rates (Padilla, Tansey and Chen 2022).
- Sparse deep ReLU network posteriors (Polson and Ročková 2018; Wang and Ročková 2020), misspecification (Kleijn and van der Vaart 2006), deep Gaussian process priors (Finocchio and Schmidt Hieber 2023; Abraham and Deo 2023), Langevin algorithms (Nickl and Wang 2024).

- [Transfer of Bernstein von Mises guarantees to the GBC posterior (BvM for GBC)](../RESULTS.md#bvm_for_gbc) (Theorem 16.2, page 275; proved): If the posterior produced by a generative Bayesian computation (GBC) generator is closer to the exact posterior than the root N scale in Wasserstein distance and concentrates on the localization set with bounded moments, then it inherits the Bernstein von Mises limit of the exact posterior for the initial condition and, for the reaction diffusion and two dimensional Navier Stokes equations, for the trajectory, with exact asymptotic coverage of its credible bands and, for Navier Stokes, attainment of the local asymptotic minimax bound for bounded losses; nothing is assumed about how the generator was trained. - [No uniform guarantee: minimax lower bound for conditional quantile generators over monotone Hölder classes](../RESULTS.md#no_uniform_guarantee_for_quantile_generators) (Theorem 16.15, page 290; proved): Any generator, trained on M simulated pairs, that must be accurate at a fixed interior summary value for every conditional law in a monotone Hölder class of conditional quantile maps of smoothness beta on a J dimensional summary has, for some map in the class, Wasserstein error at least a constant free of J times (C M) to the power minus beta over (2 beta plus J) with probability at least 1/8, where C bounds the design density, so root N accuracy uniformly over the class requires at least N to the power 1 plus J over (2 beta) simulations divided by C, more than any polynomial once J grows; reading this as the accuracy condition of the transfer theorem uses the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption. New in this book.
- [The minimax exponent for conditional quantile generators is attained for Hölder smoothness at most one (histogram generator)](../RESULTS.md#quantile_generator_exponent_attained_for_beta_at_most_one) (Proposition 16.16, page 293; proved): For Hölder smoothness at most one, the generator that reports the empirical law of the simulated parameters whose summaries fall in the same cube of a grid of side M to the power minus 1 over (2 beta plus J) has Wasserstein risk at most a constant times J to the power beta over 2 times M to the power minus beta over (2 beta plus J), uniformly over the class and the summary value, so the summary dimension J, and not J plus one, governs the rate. New in this book.
- [The minimax exponent for conditional quantile generators is attained for smoothness between one and two by jittered local linear quantile regression](../RESULTS.md#quantile_generator_exponent_attained_for_beta_between_one_and_two) (Proposition 16.19, page 295; proved): For Hölder smoothness between one and two, local linear quantile regression on a Euclidean window around an interior summary value, applied level by level to responses with a small uniform jitter and clipped to [minus 1, 1], has Wasserstein risk at most a constant times M to the power minus beta over (2 beta plus J) uniformly over the monotone Hölder class, matching the lower bound exponent even though the conditional laws may have atoms. New in this book.
- [A local linear quantile fit is nearly monotone in its error (deterministic lemma for monotone Hölder quantile maps)](../RESULTS.md#a_quantile_fit_is_nearly_monotone_in_its_error) (Lemma 16.21, page 296; proved): For a monotone Hölder conditional quantile map of smoothness between one and two, the excess level of an affine fit at a rescaled design point near an interior summary value has the sign of the fit's error, is ordered like that error, and is bounded below by two thirds of it, all up to a term of order h to the power beta. New in this book.
- [Moment conditions force a small local linear quantile fit](../RESULTS.md#moment_conditions_force_a_small_fit) (Lemma 16.22, page 298; proved): If the excess level of an affine quantile fit has small mean and small first moments under a bounded design density on the unit ball, as the first order conditions of local linear quantile regression give, then, at every level whose distance to 0 and to 1 exceeds a multiple of the moment bound, the fit is uniformly close to the first order Taylor polynomial of the true conditional quantile, with explicit constants. New in this book.
- [The minimax exponent for conditional quantile generators is attained for smoothness above two when the conditional densities are bounded](../RESULTS.md#quantile_generator_exponent_attained_for_beta_above_two_with_bounded_densities) (Proposition 16.25, page 302; proved): For Hölder smoothness above two and conditional quantile maps whose slope in the level is bounded below, so that the conditional laws have bounded densities and no atoms, local polynomial quantile regression on a window around an interior summary value has Wasserstein risk at most a constant times M to the power minus beta over (2 beta plus J), which makes beta over (2 beta plus J) the minimax exponent at an interior point for every beta above one when the lower bound on the slope is at most 1/8. - [A local polynomial quantile fit is uniformly small when the conditional densities are bounded](../RESULTS.md#a_polynomial_quantile_fit_when_the_densities_are_bounded) (Lemma 16.26, page 303; proved): When the conditional quantile maps have slope in the level bounded below, a polynomial fit whose excess level has small moments up to its degree under a design density bounded below on the unit ball is, at every level whose distance to 0 and to 1 exceeds a multiple of the moment bound, uniformly close to the Taylor polynomial of the true conditional quantile, with explicit constants depending on the degree and the dimension. New in this book.
- [No two point lower bound gives a smaller exponent for conditional quantile generators](../RESULTS.md#no_two_point_bound_gives_a_smaller_exponent) (Proposition 16.27, page 304; proved): For any two conditional quantile maps in the monotone Hölder class, the squared Hellinger distance between the laws of one training pair is at least a constant times the Wasserstein separation of their conditional laws at the summary value raised to the power 2 plus J over beta, so no two point lower bound can give an exponent smaller than beta over (2 beta plus J). New in this book.
- [Posterior contraction for the initial condition of a PDE under a sparse deep ReLU network (Polson Ročková) prior](../RESULTS.md#contraction_under_a_network_prior) (Theorem 16.28, page 306; proved): If the forward map satisfies Conditions 2A, 2B and 2E of Nickl on sup norm balls, a strengthening that the book says has not been checked for any forward map, then under the Polson Ročková spike and slab prior on sparse deep ReLU networks for the initial condition of a time evolution equation the posterior contracts around a truth of Hölder smoothness alpha, between d/2 plus 1 and the input dimension p of the networks, at the rate N to the power minus alpha over (2 alpha plus p), up to a logarithmic factor, for the forward map in L2 and for the initial condition in the negative Sobolev norm of order one, adaptively in the smoothness; an L2 rate follows only when the sieve's sup norm plus Lipschitz bound is small enough, and the book's proof is a sketch. New in this book.
- [Exactness of the one step summary in the linear Gaussian model](../RESULTS.md#exactness_of_the_one_step_summary) (Lemma 16.32, page 310; proved): In the linear Gaussian model the regularized one step summary, evaluated at any linearization point, equals the posterior mean exactly, so it does not depend on the point at which it is computed and training under a proposal other than the prior does not change its target. New in this book.
- [Residual law under a localized training proposal: prior fraction mean and variance inflation](../RESULTS.md#residual_law_under_a_localized_proposal) (Theorem 16.33, page 310; proved): In the linear Gaussian model, when training parameters are drawn from a Gaussian proposal centered at a preliminary estimate with covariance c squared times the posterior covariance, the residual of the exact summary has mean equal to the prior shrinkage of the proposal center, independent of c, and, when the prior covariance commutes with the information, per eigendirection of the prior fraction operator, variance equal to the posterior variance times s plus c squared p squared, which gives the exact bias of uncentered heads and the standard deviation error of unrescaled heads. New in this book.
- [The localized GBC design (center and rescale) meets the accuracy conditions (G1) and (G2) in the linear Gaussian model](../RESULTS.md#localized_design_meets_gbc_accuracy_conditions) (Corollary 16.37, page 315; proved): In the linear Gaussian model with a prior that commutes with the information, the localized GBC algorithm, which draws training parameters around a preliminary estimate, centers the residuals and rescales them by the exact factor of the prior fraction, reproduces the exact posterior in population at every radius and center, and, when the posterior variance of each mode is at most of the order of its Laplace eigenvalue over N, as for the heat and Stokes equations, with M simulations on J modes is within order N to the power minus one half times the square root of J over M (plus J over M) of it in Wasserstein distance, so the transfer theorem applies when J over M tends to zero. New in this book.
- [Gram matrix of the whitened weights of the one step training summary](../RESULTS.md#the_gram_matrix_of_the_whitened_weights) (Lemma 16.41, page 319; proved): The Gram matrix of the whitened weights of the one step training summary equals the compression of the inverse whitened information plus an explicit positive Schur complement term, so it dominates that compression, which is bounded below by the inverse norm of the information, and differs from it by at most the squared norm of the tail of the compact correction divided by the cube of the lower bound. New in this book.
- [No sub Gaussian envelope uniform in the dimension for the whole whitened training residual with fresh designs](../RESULTS.md#no_sub_gaussian_envelope_for_the_whole_residual) (Proposition 16.42, page 319; proved): When each simulated data set has its own random design, no law of the training parameters gives the whole whitened residual of the one step training summary a sub Gaussian norm bounded uniformly in the summary dimension: the squared sub Gaussian norm is at least twice the envelope of the whitened weights, which grows at least linearly in the dimension when the whitened information operator is bounded, and for the heat and Stokes equations grows like the J th Laplace eigenvalue in the last coordinate. New in this book.
- [The whitened training residual assumption (U2') holds under a localized training proposal](../RESULTS.md#whitened_residual_assumption_under_a_localized_proposal) (Theorem 16.43, page 320; proved): For training parameters drawn from a Gaussian proposal localized at a preliminary estimate with covariance of the posterior scale, and the one step training summary linearized there once, the whitened training residuals split into a part with sub Gaussian norm bounded uniformly in the dimension and second moment converging to the whitened inverse information, plus a remainder whose second moment tends to zero, for the heat and Stokes equations and, under three conditions on the linearization, for a nonlinear forward map. New in this book.
- [Linearization conditions (I1) to (I4) for the reaction diffusion class](../RESULTS.md#the_linearization_of_the_reaction_diffusion_class) (Lemma 16.44, page 324; proved): For Nickl's reaction diffusion class with a smooth truth, the linearized solution map satisfies the four conditions on the linearization used to prove the whitened residual assumption under localized training (coercive whitened information with compact correction, Lipschitz dependence on the linearization point, sup norm bounds and a quadratic remainder), with an information bound depending only on the sup norm of the derivative of the reaction term and the time horizon. New in this book.
- [Linearization conditions (I1) to (I4) for the two dimensional Navier Stokes equations](../RESULTS.md#the_linearization_of_the_navier_stokes_equations) (Lemma 16.45, page 325; proved): For the two dimensional periodic Navier Stokes equations with forcing in the first divergence free Sobolev space and initial condition in the second, the linearized solution map satisfies the Lipschitz, sup norm and quadratic remainder conditions on the linearization with Sobolev index two, and for smooth forcing and truth every hypothesis on the forward map of the theorem on whitened residuals under localized training holds, with the conditions of Konen and Nickl in place of Nickl's Condition 2. New in this book.
- [Centered and rescaled training residuals for a nonlinear forward map with a reused design](../RESULTS.md#centered_and_rescaled_residuals_for_a_nonlinear_forward_map) (Proposition 16.47, page 327; proved): For a nonlinear forward map, when the simulated data sets reuse the observed design and the regularized one step summary is formed at a preliminary estimate, the centered and rescaled whitened residuals of the localized GBC algorithm satisfy the analogue of the whitened residual assumption with the identity as limit covariance, and with J over M tending to zero the algorithm's head, centered at the observed summary, is within smaller order than the root N scale of the Gaussian with that center and the regularized posterior covariance. New in this book.
