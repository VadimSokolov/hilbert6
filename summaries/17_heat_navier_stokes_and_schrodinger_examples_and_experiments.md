# Chapter 17. Heat, Navier Stokes and Schrödinger: Examples and Experiments

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *On Hilbert's 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 17. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *On Hilbert's 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation*, edition 1, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23407620. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/hilbert6/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/hilbert6.
<!-- cite:end -->

This chapter tests the book's generative Bayesian computation (GBC) for the initial condition of an evolution equation (data assimilation) on three examples: the heat equation, two dimensional Navier Stokes about the Taylor Green vortex, and the Schrödinger equation (linear, and cubic nonlinear, NLS). For the heat equation it computes the information per Fourier mode (the dissipation barrier, or viscous curse) and proves a matrix Bernstein concentration bound for the Gram matrix of a uniform random design with the finite sample Bernstein von Mises error. Experiments compare GBC with exact posteriors, the Laplace approximation, pCN and Metropolis references. Without dissipation it computes the Bogoliubov information blocks of cubic NLS about a plane wave (modulational instability), proves linearized stability (defocusing) and stability of the NLS solution map (for bounded solutions), and proves a Bernstein von Mises transfer theorem for the linear Schrödinger equation; no Bernstein von Mises theorem is proved for cubic NLS. It links GBC to Longstaff Schwartz least squares Monte Carlo and to Storvik sufficient statistic filtering, and lists open problems.

## From the literature

Used as black boxes: the Bernstein von Mises theorems for time evolution equations (Nickl 2024) and for two dimensional Navier Stokes data assimilation (Konen and Nickl 2026). Also used: the inverse logarithmic lower bound (Nickl and Titi 2024), the local curvature condition for Langevin sampling (Nickl and Wang 2024), the matrix Bernstein inequality (Tropp 2015), the Taylor Green vortex (Taylor and Green 1937), the functional Bernstein von Mises theorem in Gaussian white noise (Castillo and Nickl 2013), Sobolev norm growth for NLS on two dimensional manifolds (Planchon, Tzvetkov and Visciglia 2017), the pCN sampler (Cotter, Roberts, Stuart and White 2013), the wave kinetic equation from cubic NLS (Deng and Hani 2023), Navier Stokes Fourier from hard spheres (Deng, Hani and Ma 2025) and gradient stability for McKean Vlasov (Castre and Nickl 2026). Connections: least squares Monte Carlo (Longstaff and Schwartz 2001; Clément, Lamberton and Protter 2002), Storvik's filter (Storvik 2002), jump diffusion filtering (Johannes, Polson and Stroud 2009) and Bayesian calibration (Kennedy and O'Hagan 2001; Higdon and coauthors 2004, 2008).

- [Gram matrix concentration and the finite N Bernstein von Mises error for the heat equation](../RESULTS.md#gram_concentration_and_the_finite_bernstein_von_mises_error) (Proposition 17.1, page 332; proved): For the heat equation observed at N uniform random points with D retained modes (eigenfunctions bounded by the square root of 2) and N at least D log D, the normalized Gram matrix of the design is within 12 times the square root of D log D over N of its limit with probability at least 1 minus 1/D, which bounds the finite N error of the Bernstein von Mises covariance. New in this book.
- [Information operator of the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation about a plane wave (Bogoliubov blocks)](../RESULTS.md#information_operator_of_nls_about_a_plane_wave) (Proposition 17.5, page 356; proved): Linearized about a constant background, the information operator of the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation is block diagonal over Fourier modes with explicit 2 by 2 blocks given by the Bogoliubov dispersion relation, equal in the defocusing case to T times the identity plus a compact perturbation that decays like one over the squared wave number and exponentially amplified along one direction in the focusing band. New in this book.
- [Determinant, trace and smallest eigenvalue of the Bogoliubov information block](../RESULTS.md#bogoliubov_block_determinant_trace_and_smallest_eigenvalue) (Lemma 17.6, page 359; proved): The determinant of each 2 by 2 Bogoliubov information block is at least T squared because the linearized flow preserves area, which gives explicit lower bounds on its smallest eigenvalue, uniform in the mode in the defocusing case, while in the focusing band the largest eigenvalue grows exponentially and the smallest tends to a finite limit. New in this book.
- [Linearized stability and the analogue of Condition 2F for the defocusing NLS plane wave](../RESULTS.md#linearized_stability_and_condition_2f_analogue_for_the_defocusing_plane_wave) (Corollary 17.7, page 362; proved): For the defocusing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation linearized at a constant background, the information operator is bounded above and below on L2 and on every Sobolev space with explicit constants uniform in the mode, which gives a linearized stability estimate and bounds, uniformly in the mode and in the time horizon, the covariance of the Gaussian measure that would be the Bernstein von Mises limit; no such theorem is proved for cubic NLS. New in this book.
- [Stability of the cubic NLS solution map](../RESULTS.md#stability_of_the_cubic_nls_solution_map) (Proposition 17.8, page 364; proved): For solutions of the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation that stay bounded in the supremum norm, the L2 distance of two solutions is bounded above and below by exponential factors times the distance of the initial data (an elementary energy estimate), which gives a stability estimate for the solution map on balls of H1 in one dimension and of H2 in two dimensions, with a smallness condition in the focusing two dimensional case; the supremum norm bound in the defocusing two dimensional case is Theorem 1.1 of Planchon, Tzvetkov and Visciglia (2017). New in this book.
- [Linear Schrödinger transfer: exact posterior and Bernstein von Mises theorem](../RESULTS.md#linear_schrodinger_transfer) (Proposition 17.10, page 366; proved): For the linear Schrödinger equation with a truncated Gaussian prior, the posterior is exactly Gaussian with the adjoint summary sufficient, and when the number of modes grows suitably the posterior and the posterior mean (for a smooth enough truth) satisfy a Bernstein von Mises theorem in negative Sobolev spaces with complex white noise as the limit, for the initial condition and for the state at every time but not in the supremum norm. New in this book.
- [The transfer theorem for the linear Schrödinger equation](../RESULTS.md#the_transfer_theorem_for_the_linear_schrodinger_equation) (Corollary 17.11, page 370; proved): If a GBC posterior approximates the exact posterior in Wasserstein distance on a negative Sobolev space with an error of smaller order than one over the square root of N, then for the linear Schrödinger equation it has the Gaussian limits for the parameter and for the state at every time, for Hadamard differentiable functionals, for credible balls, and efficiency of linear functionals. New in this book.
- [Beyond the plane wave: information operator of cubic NLS about a multimode background](../RESULTS.md#beyond_the_plane_wave) (Open Question 17.12, page 371; open): Open question: extend the exact information operator of cubic NLS from a constant background to a multimode background by separating a part supported on the four wave resonant manifold, decide when it is uniformly invertible, and prove a Bernstein von Mises theorem for a fixed time horizon in the focusing case. New in this book.
- [LSM is GBC for optimal stopping; GBC is LSM for the inverse problem (Longstaff Schwartz dictionary)](../RESULTS.md#lsm_gbc_dictionary_for_optimal_stopping_and_the_inverse_problem) (Proposition 17.13, page 372; proved): Longstaff Schwartz least squares regression for American options and the regression step of GBC are the same construction under a dictionary between paths and prior draws, state and summary, payoff and parameter, and continuation value and posterior mean, so that a linear basis is exact for the linear Gaussian model; the almost sure convergence of the regression coefficients is the strong law for least squares, as in the consistency of least squares Monte Carlo (Clément, Lamberton and Protter 2002), and the dictionary is what the book adds. New in this book.
- [Two limit convergence of the regression summary (least squares Monte Carlo and GBC)](../RESULTS.md#two_limit_convergence_of_the_regression_summary) (Theorem 17.14, page 373; conditional on the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption (Assumption U), in case (c) only): The regression step of GBC, least squares of the parameter on a basis of the summary, converges to the posterior mean given the summary as the number of simulations and then the number of basis functions grow, and jointly for a bounded basis (consistency of least squares is classical, Györfi and coauthors 2002, and the book gives a direct proof of the joint limit for a bounded basis); what the book adds is the identification of the limit: it is the exact posterior mean for the posterior mean summary and for the linear adjoint statistic, but only up to an error of smaller order than one over the square root of N for the one step summary, where it also depends on an unproved uniformity assumption. New in this book.
- [The adjoint statistic is a Storvik statistic](../RESULTS.md#the_adjoint_statistic_is_a_storvik_statistic) (Proposition 17.15, page 376; proved): In the linear model the adjoint statistic and the Gram matrix of the first n observations are updated recursively, the Gram matrix by a rank one update, so sequential parameter learning in the style of Storvik is exact, with particles drawn independently from the Gaussian posterior and carrying no weights. New in this book.
- [Bernstein von Mises under a network prior: the Gaussian limit of the posterior of the initial condition under the Polson Ročková prior](../RESULTS.md#bernstein_von_mises_under_a_network_prior) (Open Question 17.17, page 380; open): Open question: extend the semiparametric Bernstein von Mises theorem of Wang and Ročková (2020) to the inverse problem, that is, prove the Bernstein von Mises theorem of Nickl (2024) for the initial condition of a time evolution equation with the Gaussian series prior replaced by the Polson Ročková sparse deep ReLU network prior, uniformly over the test functions of the functional limit in a Sobolev ball, by absorbing the perturbation direction of Nickl's proof into the last layer of the network. The book proves only posterior contraction rates for this prior; with the open result, generative Bayesian computation under this adaptive prior would inherit all the conclusions of the book's transfer theorem. New in this book.
- [Uniformity: the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption and related open hypotheses](../RESULTS.md#uniformity) (Open Question 17.18, page 380; open): Open question: prove the uniform Bernstein von Mises assumption (Assumption U) on which the book's conditional theorems rest, and the uniform moment bound used for the four approximate Bayesian computation criteria. The book's question also lists related hypotheses it leaves unproved: the training residual assumption for fresh designs, and a comparison of the Gaussian centered at the summary with the posterior for a nonlinear forward map; the error bound of the one step summary for other nonlinear forward maps; a weaker regularity requirement than $\alpha>12$ for Navier Stokes; the condition of the sufficiency gap proposition for nonlinear forward maps; the expected information gain outside the reaction diffusion class, and the realized information gain formula for a growing number of modes; and whether the exponent of the theorem that no uniform guarantee holds is attained for $\beta>2$. New in this book.
- [Relinearized summary: removing the term in the fourth power of the localization radius by iterating the Gauss Newton step](../RESULTS.md#relinearized_summary) (Open Question 17.19, page 380; open): Open question: confirm that iterating the Gauss Newton step, with the Jacobian recomputed from the data alone at each iterate, removes the term in the fourth power of the localization radius from the book's error decomposition for localized generative Bayesian computation, find how fast that term decays with the number of steps, and determine whether, in a nonlinear problem, the resulting error falls below the error of the Laplace approximation. The book reports a numerical confirmation for a two step summary in a two dimensional Navier Stokes experiment. New in this book.
- [Finite N: the non Gaussian correction for the Taylor Green problem and a nonasymptotic bound on the GBC posterior error](../RESULTS.md#finite_n) (Open Question 17.20, page 381; open): Open question: the transfer of the Bernstein von Mises limit to the generative Bayesian computation (GBC) posterior is a root N statement, and the book's two finite N checks, a linear heat equation example and a six mode Navier Stokes experiment, are not quantifications of the finite N behavior. Quantify the non Gaussian correction for the Taylor Green problem as a function of the ratio of the vortex amplitude to the viscosity and of N, and bound the Wasserstein distance between the GBC posterior and the exact posterior nonasymptotically in terms of the generator approximation error for the true conditional quantile map; the book's experiments did not find, in any regime tested, the crossover at which non Gaussianity would favor a simulation based method over the Laplace approximation, so the answer cannot be assumed. New in this book.
- [Model error: the rate at which the discrepancy amplitude must vanish for the deterministic limit to persist](../RESULTS.md#model_error) (Open Question 17.21, page 381; open): Open question: combine the book's transfer theorem for generative Bayesian computation with a discrepancy process whose amplitude tends to zero with the number of observations, and identify the rate at which the amplitude must tend to zero for the deterministic limit to persist, connecting to the Gibbs and particle smoothers for state space models. The book's theorems on the initial condition problem assume deterministic dynamics, and a trajectory generated with model error does not lie on the solution manifold on which the posterior lives. New in this book.
